What 3 million calls to 311 say about New York

I took a full year of public 311 service requests from NYC Open Data, modelled it in PostgreSQL and asked the questions a city operations team would ask: when do requests arrive, what people complain about and how that changes with the seasons, how long each agency takes to close them, where they come from and how people reach the city.

How it's built

The whole process rebuilds from scratch with one command (./scripts/load.sh).

  1. StagingThe 12 monthly CSV files, downloaded from the public API, load as plain text with \copy, so an odd value never stops the load.
  2. CoreTyped model with primary keys, foreign keys and CHECKs. Nothing is dropped for being implausible; the raw facts stay.
  3. ChecksEvery load runs data-quality checks and fails if any says REVIEW.
  4. MartA materialized view with one row per request and a flag for each problem: closed before created, bad ZIP, no coordinates, possible duplicate.
  5. AnalysisWindow functions, percentiles, a generated calendar, seasonal normalisation per day and a lat/long grid over millions of rows.
  6. PublicationOnly aggregates leave the database, as one JSON that this page draws without external libraries and without map tiles.

Data: 311 Service Requests from 2010 to Present, published by the City of New York on NYC Open Data under its Terms of Use. This page publishes aggregates only: no individual request, address or description.

Times are local to New York City (the source has no time zone). The source is updated daily, so closing dates of requests created in 2024 keep changing: figures here correspond to the day the data was loaded.
